01 Higher Education Curriculum Strategy

Grand Canyon University - CST: Building Three Degree Programs from Concept to Curriculum

I collaborated with university leadership, instructional designers, and subject-matter experts to design three technology degree programs— from early program concepts through curriculum mapping, course design, and industry-aligned learning experiences.

Hover or click to read more Close case study ↘
Subject Matter Expert • Curriculum Strategy • Higher Education

Transforming industry demand into accredited degree programs

As a Subject Matter Expert, I collaborated with Grand Canyon University leadership, instructional designers, fellow subject matter experts, and curriculum teams to develop three new technology degree programs from the ground up. Our work began long before individual courses existed—defining program vision, graduate competencies, and curriculum architecture that would prepare students for careers in the rapidly evolving fields of game development, game design, and human-computer interaction.

Working across multiple disciplines required balancing industry expectations, accreditation requirements, learner progression, assessment strategy, and technical depth while ensuring every course contributed meaningfully to the overall program outcomes.

End-to-end curriculum design

I contributed throughout the entire curriculum development lifecycle, from initial program concept and competency mapping through detailed course planning, assessment design, and development of multiple production-ready university courses.

Curriculum development process

Rather than designing isolated courses, our team first established the complete learning journey students would experience over multiple years. This included identifying graduate competencies, sequencing knowledge and skills across the curriculum, defining prerequisite relationships, and ensuring every course advanced students toward professional readiness.

My contributions

  • Defined program-level learning outcomes and graduate competencies.
  • Mapped competencies across complete degree pathways.
  • Designed curriculum progression from foundational to advanced coursework.
  • Developed detailed course maps and instructional blueprints.
  • Created multiple university courses and learning experiences.
  • Designed authentic assessments aligned with industry practice.
  • Worked closely with instructional designers to translate technical content into engaging online learning experiences.
  • Reviewed curriculum for technical accuracy, coherence, and alignment across programs.

Programs I supported

What made this project meaningful

This project combined my experience as a software engineer, educator, and instructional designer. Rather than simply writing instructional content, I helped shape how future students would progress through entire degree programs—from their first introductory concepts to portfolio-ready projects and advanced technical skills.

Seeing those programs launch and later teaching within the same ecosystem provided a unique perspective on the complete curriculum lifecycle, allowing me to understand how strategic curriculum decisions ultimately influence real student learning outcomes.

    From strategy to execution

    Curriculum succeeds when every part of the system works together.

    Outcomes, assessments, technology, faculty guidance, learner support, and implementation must reinforce one another—not compete for attention.

    How I Work

    Curriculum is a system—not a collection of assignments.

    I design across the complete learning ecosystem, connecting institutional goals, learner needs, faculty usability, technical accuracy, assessment evidence, and long-term program sustainability.

    01 · Discover

    Understand the real need

    Clarify learner context, performance gaps, stakeholder priorities, technical constraints, and measures of success.

    02 · Architect

    Build the learning system

    Map outcomes, competencies, course sequences, assessments, projects, and instructional support into a coherent structure.

    03 · Develop

    Create usable curriculum

    Write clear content, authentic projects, rubrics, faculty guidance, and learner-facing experiences that work in practice.

    04 · Improve

    Learn from execution

    Use instructor experience, learner performance, feedback, and quality review to continuously strengthen the program.
