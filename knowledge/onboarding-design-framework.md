# Onboarding Design Framework

## Overview

Jamie Reichenberger designs onboarding as a structured learning experience that helps people move from uncertainty to confidence, competence, and independent performance.

Her onboarding framework is designed for:

- Employees
- Instructors
- Students
- Customers
- Software users
- Technical teams
- Program participants
- Contractors
- Volunteers

Jamie does not treat onboarding as a single orientation meeting or a collection of documents.

She treats onboarding as a guided performance journey.

The goal is to help a person understand:

- Where they are
- What is expected
- What they need to do first
- Which tools and systems they must use
- Where to find help
- How success will be measured
- How to become increasingly independent

# Onboarding Philosophy

## Reduce Uncertainty First

People entering a new environment often experience uncertainty before they experience difficulty.

They may be wondering:

- Am I in the right place?
- What should I do first?
- Who should I ask for help?
- What happens if I make a mistake?
- What am I expected to know already?
- How will I know whether I am doing well?

Jamie designs onboarding to answer these questions early.

Clear expectations reduce anxiety and allow people to focus on learning.

## Build Confidence Through Early Wins

Jamie intentionally designs small, achievable tasks at the beginning of onboarding.

Examples include:

- Logging into a required system
- Completing a profile
- Sending a first message
- Submitting a sample assignment
- Completing a guided customer interaction
- Making a first code commit
- Teaching a short practice activity
- Completing a simple workflow

Early wins help people feel capable and create momentum.

## Deliver Information When It Is Needed

Jamie avoids overwhelming people with every policy, process, and tool on the first day.

Instead, she organizes onboarding around just-in-time learning.

People receive information when they are ready to use it.

For example:

- Account setup information is delivered before system access is required.
- Grading guidance is delivered before the first assignment is graded.
- Customer escalation procedures are introduced before independent support work begins.
- Advanced software features are introduced after basic navigation is complete.

## Design for Performance, Not Completion

Completing an onboarding checklist does not necessarily mean a person is ready to perform.

Jamie evaluates whether the person can:

- Use required tools
- Follow key workflows
- Make appropriate decisions
- Communicate effectively
- Complete essential tasks
- Find answers independently
- Recognize when escalation is required

# Common Onboarding Problems

Traditional onboarding often fails because it includes:

- Too much information at once
- Long presentations
- Unorganized documentation
- Unclear expectations
- Missing examples
- No opportunity to practice
- No feedback
- No role-specific learning
- No progress milestones
- No follow-up after the first week
- No clear owner of the onboarding experience

Jamie addresses these problems by creating a staged, measurable onboarding journey.

# Jamie's Onboarding Design Framework

Jamie organizes onboarding into six stages:

1. Prepare
2. Welcome
3. Orient
4. Practice
5. Perform
6. Reinforce

# Stage 1: Prepare

## Purpose

Preparation begins before the person officially starts.

The goal is to remove preventable barriers and make the first experience feel organized.

## Preparation Activities

Examples include:

- Creating accounts
- Assigning system permissions
- Providing login instructions
- Confirming equipment needs
- Sending a welcome message
- Sharing the onboarding schedule
- Assigning a mentor or point of contact
- Preparing role-specific materials
- Confirming required forms
- Identifying accessibility needs

## Preparation Questions

Jamie may ask:

- What must be ready before Day 1?
- Which systems require advance approval?
- What information does the person need before starting?
- Who is responsible for each setup task?
- What could prevent the person from participating?
- What does the manager, instructor, or customer success team need to prepare?

## Preparation Deliverables

Possible deliverables include:

- Preboarding checklist
- Account setup checklist
- Welcome email
- First-day agenda
- Technology requirements
- Contact directory
- Manager preparation guide
- Instructor preparation guide

# Stage 2: Welcome

## Purpose

The welcome stage establishes belonging, purpose, and psychological safety.

A person should understand why they are here and how their work or participation connects to a larger goal.

## Welcome Content

Examples include:

- Mission and purpose
- Team or program overview
- Role expectations
- Key people
- Communication norms
- Learning roadmap
- Support channels
- First-week goals

## Effective Welcome Experience

A strong welcome experience should answer:

- What is this organization or program trying to accomplish?
- How does my role contribute?
- What should I expect during onboarding?
- Who will support me?
- What should I complete first?
- Where can I ask questions?

# Stage 3: Orient

## Purpose

Orientation introduces the environment, tools, terminology, policies, and workflows required for participation.

## Orientation Topics

Depending on the audience, orientation may include:

- Organizational structure
- Program structure
- Role responsibilities
- Required technology
- Communication tools
- File organization
- Security practices
- Policies
- Customer standards
- Academic expectations
- Escalation procedures
- Performance standards

## Orientation Design Principles

Jamie keeps orientation:

- Role-specific
- Clearly sequenced
- Easy to navigate
- Searchable
- Supported by examples
- Connected to upcoming tasks

## Orientation Deliverables

Examples include:

- Interactive onboarding guide
- Quick-start guide
- Role-based learning path
- System walkthroughs
- Process maps
- Glossary
- Frequently asked questions
- Short knowledge checks
- Video demonstrations
- Reference sheets

# Stage 4: Practice

## Purpose

The practice stage allows people to apply new knowledge in a supported environment.

This is where onboarding shifts from information delivery to skill development.

## Practice Activities

Examples include:

- Simulations
- Guided tasks
- Sample assignments
- Practice customer conversations
- Mock teaching sessions
- Software sandbox activities
- Role-playing
- Case studies
- Troubleshooting exercises
- Code-along activities
- Shadowing
- Peer review

## Guided Practice Structure

Jamie often structures practice as:

1. Observe an example
2. Complete a guided version
3. Receive feedback
4. Repeat with reduced support
5. Complete a similar task independently

## Safe Practice

Practice should allow people to make mistakes without serious consequences.

Examples include:

- Drafting a customer response before sending it
- Grading a sample assignment before grading live work
- Completing a mock CRM workflow
- Using sample data
- Practicing in a test environment
- Conducting a simulated support call
- Presenting to a mentor before presenting to stakeholders

# Stage 5: Perform

## Purpose

The performance stage transitions the person into real work or independent participation.

Support remains available, but responsibility gradually increases.

## Performance Milestones

Examples include:

- Complete the first independent task
- Lead the first meeting
- Teach the first class
- Respond to the first live customer
- Complete the first project
- Grade the first assignment
- Build the first production feature
- Complete the first reporting cycle

## Readiness Criteria

Jamie defines what a person must demonstrate before moving into independent performance.

Readiness may include the ability to:

- Complete essential workflows
- Use required tools correctly
- Follow quality standards
- Communicate appropriately
- Identify common errors
- Find documentation
- Ask for help when needed
- Escalate high-risk issues
- Meet expected timelines

## Gradual Release of Responsibility

Jamie may use a progression such as:

- Observe
- Assist
- Perform with supervision
- Perform with review
- Perform independently
- Support others

# Stage 6: Reinforce

## Purpose

Onboarding does not end after the first task or first week.

The reinforcement stage strengthens knowledge, addresses gaps, and supports long-term performance.

## Reinforcement Activities

Examples include:

- Check-in meetings
- Refresher training
- Performance feedback
- Advanced learning modules
- Coaching
- Peer support
- Office hours
- Knowledge-base recommendations
- Practice scenarios
- Progress reviews
- 30-, 60-, and 90-day evaluations

## Reinforcement Questions

Jamie may evaluate:

- Which skills are still developing?
- Where does the person need more support?
- Which workflows create confusion?
- Are expectations clear?
- Is the person becoming more independent?
- Are managers or instructors providing consistent support?
- Which onboarding materials should be improved?

# Onboarding Journey Map

A typical onboarding journey may include the following stages.

## Before Day 1

Goals:

- Remove access barriers
- Set expectations
- Reduce anxiety
- Prepare required systems

Possible activities:

- Welcome email
- Account setup
- Required forms
- Technology check
- Schedule review
- Introduction to a mentor

## Day 1

Goals:

- Build belonging
- Explain the purpose
- Clarify immediate expectations
- Complete a simple first task

Possible activities:

- Welcome meeting
- Team or program introduction
- Learning roadmap
- Communication norms
- First guided task
- Support-channel introduction

## Week 1

Goals:

- Build foundational knowledge
- Introduce tools and workflows
- Create early wins
- Begin guided practice

Possible activities:

- System walkthroughs
- Process demonstrations
- Sample tasks
- Knowledge checks
- Mentor check-in
- Reflection

## Weeks 2 Through 4

Goals:

- Increase task complexity
- Provide feedback
- Reduce support gradually
- Begin real performance

Possible activities:

- Supervised work
- Milestone assignments
- Role-specific scenarios
- Feedback sessions
- First independent task

## Days 30 Through 60

Goals:

- Strengthen consistency
- Address gaps
- Expand responsibility
- Introduce advanced skills

Possible activities:

- Performance review
- Advanced learning
- Cross-functional exposure
- Independent projects
- Targeted coaching

## Days 60 Through 90

Goals:

- Demonstrate independence
- Confirm long-term readiness
- Establish continued development goals
- Gather onboarding feedback

Possible activities:

- Final onboarding review
- Skills demonstration
- Development plan
- Process feedback
- Advanced role expectations

Jamie identifies the shortest path between account creation and meaningful value.

For example:

- A CRM user imports the first contact and launches the first workflow.
- A school administrator creates the first class.
- A customer places the first order successfully.
- A creator receives and understands campaign instructions.
- A software user completes the first automated task.

# Summary

Jamie’s onboarding design framework moves people through six stages:

1. Prepare
2. Welcome
3. Orient
4. Practice
5. Perform
6. Reinforce

The framework is designed to:

- Reduce uncertainty
- Prevent information overload
- Build confidence
- Create early wins
- Develop real skills
- Support independent performance
- Improve consistency
- Reduce avoidable errors
- Strengthen long-term retention and adoption

Jamie designs onboarding as a complete experience rather than a one-time event.
